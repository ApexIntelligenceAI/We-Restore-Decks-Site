Site-Wide FAQ

Questions Homeowners Ask Most

Use this page as a quick reference before requesting your estimate.

How do I know if my deck should be repaired, restored, remodeled, or replaced?

It depends on structure condition, age, and your goals. If framing is still sound, repair, restoration, or remodeling is often possible. If structural deterioration is widespread, replacement may be the safer long-term path.

What is typically included in a top-deck replacement/remodel scope?

Typical scope can include old decking removal, frame inspection, added framing/flashing where needed, surface replacement, railing updates, and cleanup/haul-away. Final details depend on your deck’s condition and layout.

When does deck restoration make more sense than resurfacing?

Restoration is usually a fit when the deck is still structurally healthy and mainly needs cleaning, prep, sanding, and stain protection. Resurfacing is usually better when top boards are heavily worn, splintery, or near end-of-life.

How do I choose between oil stain, solid stain, and semi-transparent stain?

Oil stains are often great for clean/new wood, solid stains are common on previously coated surfaces, and semi-transparent non-oil systems allow wood grain to show while adding color/protection.

How long do stain finishes usually last?

Durability varies by stain type, exposure, and maintenance. As a general guide from your site content, solid systems can last longer on verticals than flats, and semi-transparent coatings usually need more frequent refreshes on deck floors.

Do you clean siding/decks with low pressure or high pressure?

Low-pressure methods are used for house surfaces and delicate materials, while high-pressure cleaning is typically used for hardscape like patios, driveways, and masonry where appropriate.

Can vinyl and composite deck surfaces be cleaned safely?

Yes. Controlled pressure and suitable detergents are used to remove mold/algae/deposits while reducing risk of cutting or marring the surface.

What are signs my dock needs resurfacing or restoration?

Common signs include splintering, weathered boards, rough walking surfaces, and age-related wear. Heavier surface wear usually points to resurfacing; lighter wear with solid structure may fit restoration.

How much more does a new deck usually cost versus remodel?

From your pricing page guidance, a new build is often roughly 35%-50% higher than remodel scope because of added planning and construction steps. Actual cost depends on layout, rail choices, and condition findings.

Can I get an estimate before an on-site visit?

Yes. You can share photos, measurements, and project notes for an initial range, then finalize scope/pricing after on-site inspection and condition review.
